================================================================
READING THE RESULTS HONESTLY
================================================================

Every one of the 17 mutants was DETECTED (exit 1, at least one named
assertion failing). Three of them failed only the FIRST assertion of their
arm, and that is worth stating rather than glossing:

- `corrupt`: with `M10.corrupt` removed from `m8SoftRecoveryAuth`, nothing is
  written anyway — because `m10AuthNow()` checks `M10.corrupt` BEFORE the
  storage block, so `a.why` is "corrupt", not "blocked", and the wrapper never
  reaches the exemption branch at all. The check inside `m8SoftRecoveryAuth`
  is defence in depth. The arm still detects its removal because it asserts
  the refusal REASON directly, not merely the absence of a write.
- `holder` / [changed account]: the account change makes
  `ownershipAmbiguous()` true, so `trainingQuarantined()` refuses one line
  later. Again defence in depth; again detected by the reason assertion.
- `holder` / [changed session]: `m10Reset()` also zeroes the fence, so the
  fence check refuses one line later. Same.

The order of the checks inside `m8SoftRecoveryAuth` is therefore load-bearing
for the REASON reported, and several conditions are independently sufficient
to refuse. That is the intended shape of a fail-closed gate, but it means the
per-arm "persists nothing" assertions are not, on their own, proof that any
single check is necessary. The "cannot use the exemption" assertions — which
pin the exact refusal reason — are what carry that proof, and all ten of them
fail when their guard is removed.

The one mutant that removes the exemption entirely (`noexempt`) fails T17a on
all three of its assertions, which is the direct evidence that T17a is
measuring the new code path and not something that was already true.
